## What Went Well

### 1. Execution Mode Shift
Epic 2 flowed with significantly less friction than Epic 1. The team shifted from "discussing what to do" to "executing, closing, documenting, and moving on." This is the key inflection point - **when the process stops being a topic of conversation and becomes silent context, you're doing well.**

### 2. Debt Management Framework
The HIGH/MEDIUM/LOW classification worked effectively:
- **HIGH** → Breaks AC → Must fix
- **MEDIUM** → Improves quality → Context-dependent
- **LOW** → Polish/coherence → Document only

The key insight: **"La deuda no es técnica, es temporal."** The question isn't "is it wrong?" but "is it the right moment?"

### 3. Separation of Truths
Instead of forcing everything to match perfectly, we accepted:
- **Git** = truth of code
- **Stories** = truth of product
- **Retro/decisions** = truth of process

This is organizational architecture, not just software. When each thing has its role, the system scales.

### 4. Honest History
Multiple opportunities arose to rewrite commits, recreate branches, or "clean up" history. We didn't. An honest history is more valuable than a pretty one - it generates trust, auditability, and less paranoia in real teams.

### 5. Story 0 Sanitation Success

## What Could Be Improved

### 1. Story 2.3 Already Implemented
During Task 1 of Story 2.3 (Demo & Repository Links), we discovered the functionality was already implemented in Story 2.2. The story was completed by adding 5 unit tests for the existing implementation.

**Learning:** Pre-check story scope against what's already implemented before creating detailed task breakdown.

### 2. Structural Conflicts
Conflicts in `sprint-status.yaml` during merges continued to be a friction point. This is expected structural friction from versioning tracking files, not a bug.

**Learning:** "If a problem appears twice with the same form, it's not a bug - it's a poorly explicit rule."

## Angel's Personal Retrospective Insights

> "El objetivo no es escribir código perfecto, es crear un sistema donde las decisiones sean claras y repetibles."

### Mental Checklist for Real-World Projects

**Before starting a story:**
- What breaks the DoD?
- What will I NOT touch even if I see it?

**During the story:**
- Does what I want to fix change the contract?
- Am I adding value or seeking personal tranquility?

**When closing:**
- Can I explain in 30 seconds what was achieved?
- Is the debt visible?
- Is the system a bit easier to work with?

### On Not Rewriting History
Philosophy of pragmatic approximation - we don't spend energy perfecting history because value lies in traceability, not aesthetics. Unexpected fixes that document "how much it cost and what it solved" are valuable information. The rest is noise not worth filtering unless it's a large, out-of-scope issue.
